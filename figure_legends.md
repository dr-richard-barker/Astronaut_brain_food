# Figure Legends — Neurological Countermeasures Analysis

## Figure 1. Brain transcriptomic response to spaceflight (volcano plot)

Scatter plot of pooled log$_2$ fold change (x-axis) versus -log$_{10}$(adjusted p-value) (y-axis) for all 29,839 genes from the random-effects meta-analysis of 4 rodent ISS spaceflight brain studies (OSD-525, OSD-564, OSD-612, OSD-613). Gray dots represent all tested genes; orange dots indicate consensus upregulated genes (n=208); blue dots indicate consensus downregulated genes (n=297); gold dots with black outlines mark the 13 core genes (consistently differentially expressed across all studies). Dashed horizontal line indicates adjusted p = 0.05. Selected genes with the strongest combined effect size and significance are labeled. The asymmetry toward downregulation reflects the dominant suppression of mitochondrial and protein homeostasis pathways in the spaceflight brain.

## Figure 2. Brain GSEA pathways grouped by neurological theme (dotplot)

Horizontal bar chart showing the top 3 pathways per neurological theme (by |NES|) from combined Hallmark and Reactome GSEA of the brain consensus signature. Bar color indicates direction: orange = upregulated, blue = downregulated. Dot color indicates theme (see right margin labels). Dot size is proportional to -log$_{10}$(adjusted p-value). Themes are ordered from most downregulated (top) to most upregulated (bottom). The dominant downregulated themes — mitochondrial dysfunction, protein homeostasis, DNA damage repair, and cell cycle suppression — contrast with upregulated synaptic/neuronal signaling and circadian/cholesterol pathways, revealing a shift from energy-intensive cellular processes toward structural and signaling adaptation.

## Figure 3. Top 15 brain LINCS L1000 reversers (bar chart)

Horizontal bar chart of the 15 compounds with the most negative median connectivity z-sum from LINCS L1000 screening against the brain consensus signature. More negative z-sum indicates stronger reversal of the spaceflight brain transcriptional signature. Bar color indicates clinical phase: green = Launched, blue = Phase 2, orange = Phase 1, gray = Unknown. Phase annotations are shown on the right margin. Vemurafenib (RAF inhibitor, z=-9.68) is the top reverser, followed by chlorambucil and formoterol. Notable neuroprotective nutrients in the ranking include sulforaphane (rank 7, Phase 2, Nrf2 activator) and ascorbic acid (rank 10).

## Figure 4. Nutrient pharmacokinetic profile comparison (radar chart)

Radar chart comparing 7 brain-relevant vegan nutrients across 6 pharmacokinetic and evidence axes scored on a 0–5 scale: BBB penetration, oral bioavailability, brain concentration, evidence grade, LINCS reversal strength, and spaceflight relevance. Scores are derived from published pharmacokinetic data and LINCS L1000 connectivity results. Ascorbic acid and sulforaphane show the strongest overall profiles, with high BBB penetration, bioavailability, and spaceflight relevance. Curcumin scores low on bioavailability and brain concentration despite mechanistic appeal, reflecting the 2025 critical reappraisal of curcumin PK. Resveratrol and EGCG show moderate BBB penetration but limited oral bioavailability. Quercetin and luteolin show balanced multi-target profiles.

## Figure 5. Spaceflight brain injury mechanism cascade (schematic)

Mechanism diagram showing the four-tier causal cascade from spaceflight stressors to neurological outcomes. Tier 1 (blue): spaceflight stressors — microgravity, cosmic radiation, and circadian disruption. Tier 2 (orange): cellular stress responses — oxidative stress (elevated ROS), DNA damage (double-strand breaks), and mitochondrial dysfunction (Complex I deficit). Tier 3 (red): molecular consequences — BBB disruption (ZO-1 downregulation, AQP4/PECAM-1 upregulation), protein homeostasis failure (UPR activation), neuroinflammation (NF-kB activation, cytokine release), and synaptic signaling alterations. Tier 4 (dark red): neurological outcomes — hippocampal apoptosis (2.2-fold increase reported in 35-day ISS mission), cognitive decline, and sensorimotor impairment. Downward arrows indicate causal progression. Molecular markers are derived from spaceflight rodent brain transcriptomics and proteomics studies.

## Figure 6. Nutrient-pathway-gene intervention network (schematic)

Tripartite network diagram showing how 7 brain-protective vegan nutrients (left, green circles) target 5 spaceflight-damaged brain pathways (center, orange rectangles) to activate 4 downstream protective mechanisms (right, blue rectangles). Solid lines indicate activation; dashed lines indicate inhibition. Line colors correspond to the source nutrient. Key intervention paths: sulforaphane and EGCG activate Nrf2/ARE to counteract oxidative phosphorylation suppression; resveratrol and EGCG promote mitochondrial biogenesis via SIRT1/PGC-1a; quercetin preserves BBB integrity (ZO-1); ascorbic acid supports neurotransmitter synthesis and BBB structural integrity; luteolin and curcumin inhibit NF-kB-driven neuroinflammation. The network illustrates the multi-target, complementary nature of the proposed nutritional countermeasure strategy.

## Figure 7. Diet-neuroprotection strategy by mission phase (infographic)

Three-panel infographic showing the phased nutritional countermeasure strategy across the spaceflight mission timeline. Left panel (blue, Pre-flight, T-30 days): preconditioning phase focused on Nrf2 priming (broccoli sprouts/sulforaphane), vitamin C saturation (citrus), mitochondrial priming (green tea/EGCG), and BBB strengthening (quercetin-rich foods). Center panel (green, In-flight): continuous protection during ISS duration with daily sulforaphane, turmeric/curcumin (noting bioavailability limitations), green tea smoothies (EGCG + ascorbic acid), quercetin for BBB maintenance, and luteolin herbs for neuroinflammation control. Right panel (orange, Post-flight, T+30 days): recovery phase targeting BBB repair (cruciferous vegetables), mitochondrial recovery (ginger-green tea broth), antioxidant restoration (berry-rich breakfast), and senescent cell clearance (cocoa-chia quercetin). Arrows indicate mission timeline progression. Colored dots indicate nutrient assignments.

## Figure 8. Vegan recipe nutrient coverage for neuroprotection (heatmap)

Heatmap showing the presence (gold/orange) or absence (cream) of 10 countermeasure nutrients across 12 curated vegan recipes. Rows are recipes ordered by mission phase (blue = pre-flight, green = in-flight, orange = post-flight, shown in left margin). Columns are the 10 neuroprotective nutrients: sulforaphane, curcumin, ascorbic acid, quercetin, luteolin, resveratrol, EGCG, gingerol, allicin, and lutein. Nutrient coverage scores (right margin) quantify the total countermeasure potential of each recipe. The Broccoli Sulforaphane Power Bowl (score=143) and Spiced Chickpea and Spinach Curry (score=137) provide the broadest nutrient coverage, targeting multiple spaceflight brain injury pathways simultaneously.
